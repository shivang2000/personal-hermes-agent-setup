- [Claude produced a computer-checked formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem) — Anthropic says dozens of Claude agents generated a 13-million-line Lean development containing about 30,300 proved theorems over 11 days. The important advance is not a new mathematical proof, but autonomous translation of a large existing proof into kernel-verifiable artifacts—a promising pattern for review-heavy scientific and software work.

**AI, Agents & Models**
- [EEBench evaluates circuit-design agents with deterministic SPICE tests](https://eebench.org/blog/can-ai-design-circuit-boards-yet/) — Agents edit declarative `atopile` circuits, while the harness checks voltage behavior, tolerance corners, real component characteristics, availability and cost. This exposes failures hidden by syntactically valid CAD output—for example, nominal capacitance collapsing under DC bias—and is a useful blueprint for executable, domain-specific agent evals.
- [The “comprehension debt” risk of autonomous incident response](https://www.sylvainkalache.com/blog/ai-handles-incidents-engineers-lose-touch-with-their-systems) — As agents absorb routine diagnosis and remediation, operators lose the repetitions that build failure intuition. The practical countermeasure is recurrent simulated incidents, chaos exercises and requiring agents to preserve evidence and decision traces—not merely provide retrospective explanations.
