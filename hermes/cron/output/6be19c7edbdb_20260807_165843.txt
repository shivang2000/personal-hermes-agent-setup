- **[AMD acquires Taalas — "model-specific" inference silicon](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1296/amd-acquires-taalas-to-advance-compute-solutions-for-rapidly-growing-ai-inference-market)** ([The Register coverage](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/08/06/amd-acquires-ai-chip-startup-taalas-to-boost-inference-performance-by-etching-models-into-silicon/5284344)). Toronto-based Taalas bakes model weights directly into mask-ROM silicon (plus SRAM for KV cache / LoRA adapters) instead of loading from HBM. Their HC1 test chip on TSMC 6nm served Llama 3.1 8B at ~17,000 tok/s — 48× a GPU, 8.5× Cerebras. AMD plans to pair Instinct GPUs with Taalas accelerators inside Helios racks (likely a disaggregated prompt-on-GPU / token-on-Taalas split) for high-volume agentic inference. Tradeoff: any meaningful model change requires a metal-layer re-spin.
- **[Humans miss ~1 in 3 dangerous agent commands — 40k-game data](https://scalex.dev/blog/ai-agent-permissions-stats/)** ([HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45000000)). Across ~409k approve/deny decisions in a permission-fatigue game, mean accuracy was 66.3%; 0.4% of players just clicked approve on everything. Scope-violation commands like `cat ~/.aws/credentials` were missed 35% of the time; `npm run analyze`-style script abuse was the #1 miss at 64.7% even when the malicious payload was visible in the history log. The data argues strongly for sandboxing + context-isolated defaults instead of relying on human approval.
